# Glossary

Terms the code and comments use. CLAUDE.md lists the four basic ones (crease pattern, mountain/valley, gadget, motif); these are the rest, with the book's meaning (Lang, *Twists, Tilings, and Tessellations*) and where the project uses it.

**Flat-foldable** — the pattern can end as a flat folded state, with every fold angle ±π and no layer passing through another. Phases 3 to 5 decide this. **Rigid-foldable** — there is a continuous motion from flat to the folded state in which every facet stays rigid and only the creases bend. A stricter property, so a flat-foldable pattern can be unreachable by a rigid motion: no triangle twist is rigid (Lang §7.6.1). Phase 6's bar-and-hinge model lets the facets stretch for that reason. **Developable** — a surface with zero Gaussian curvature; at a vertex the 3D sector angles still sum to 2π (Lang Eq. 1.43).

**Sector** — the angle between two consecutive creases at a vertex, counter-clockwise; sector i opens at crease i. Sizes are compared exactly, never as angles.

**Kawasaki, Maekawa** — at a flat-foldable interior vertex the odd sectors sum to π (Kawasaki–Justin) and |#M − #V| = 2 (Maekawa–Justin; it needs the paper not to pass through itself, Lang p. 28).

**Run** — a maximal block of equal consecutive sectors, each strictly smaller than the sector before and after the block (`smallSectorRuns`). **Big-Little-Big** — the creases that bound a run are not all one assignment. Necessary at any vertex; exact up to degree 4.

**Crimp** — Hull's reduction step: a sector no larger than its neighbours, with creases of opposite assignment, is deleted along with those two creases and its neighbours merge into one sector of size θᵢ₋₁ − θᵢ + θᵢ₊₁. Repeating it decides one vertex exactly (`singleVertexFlat`, Lang §1.2.7).

**Iso, anto** — two creases at a vertex with the same (iso) or opposite (anto) assignment. A strictly small sector forces its two creases anto. An odd loop of anto relations means no assignment exists, even when Kawasaki and Maekawa hold at every vertex (Lang Thm 16; Hull's triangle, `hullTriangle`).

**Border vertex, wedge** — a vertex on the edge of the sheet. The paper there is one or more wedges between two paper edges. There is no Kawasaki or Maekawa condition, only Big-Little-Big on the sectors inside a wedge (`paperWedges`, `borderRuns`; Lang Fig. 1.45).

**Face colouring** — the facets of a flat-foldable pattern can be two-coloured so that facets across a folded crease differ; one colour is face up in the folded form (`twoColor`, Lang pp. 14–15).

**Maxwell condition** — a counting test, 2F − E + E_b − 3 ≥ 0, for a reciprocal figure to exist (`maxwellExcess`, Lang §6.4.1). Sufficient only for generic embeddings.

**Pleat** — two parallel creases, one mountain and one valley, that fold the paper into a step. The arms of a twist are pleats.

**Twist angle α** — the angle between a pleat and a side of the twist's polygon; the polygon's centre turns through 2α when the twist folds (Lang §3.4.3). **Closed-back** twist: α equals the critical angle αcrit = 90° − 180°/n for the regular n-gon (Lang Eq. 3.13), so the back has no hole. **Open-back**: α < αcrit, which leaves a shrunken, rotated copy of the polygon as a hole at the centre of the back. The shipped hexagon twist is closed-back.

**Angle defect** — 2π minus the sum of the 3D angles of the triangles round an interior vertex of a folded state; 0 for a true fold of uncut paper, positive at a cone, negative at a saddle (`angleDefect`, Lang Eq. 1.43). **Consistency (closure) residual** — ‖C − I‖ for C = Π Rₓ(γᵢ)·R_z(αᵢ), the product round a vertex of a rotation about each crease by its fold angle γᵢ and one in the panel by its flat sector angle αᵢ (`closureResidual`, `consistencyResidual`, Lang §9.4.2). With the measured angles in place of the flat ones it is the identity for any real vertex and means nothing. **Panel flex** — the fold angle of a hinge inside a panel (a triangulation diagonal): how far a panel the model treats as rigid has bent.

**Gauss map** — the unit normals of the facets of a folded pattern, as points on the sphere (`faceNormals`, Lang §8.5). A parallel pleat gives one great circle. **Major and minor folds** — at a degree-4 vertex the two opposite creases of larger fold angle are the major pair and the other two the minor pair; a flat-foldable vertex has equal angles within each pair (Lang Thms 25 and 26). **Bird's-foot vertex** — sectors (α, α, π − α, π − α), with tan(γ₊/2) = sec α · tan(γ₋/2) for the major and minor folds (`birdsFootMajor`, Lang Eq. 2.2). Its collinear pair can instead fold as one **straight crease** with the other two flat, which is what the simulation does when the pair share an assignment.

**Supercritical twist** — α above αcrit (`twistRegime`, `core/gadgets/twist-limits.ts`): neighbouring pleats would cross inside the polygon, so a cyclic assignment folds only where the paper ends early. The triangle twist (α = 60°, αcrit = 30°) is one; the hexagon twist (α = αcrit = 60°) is closed-back.

**Necklace** — a cyclic word counted up to rotation. The pleat orientations of an n-gon twist form N(n) = (1/n) Σ_{d|n} 2^d φ(n/d) necklaces (`necklaceCount`, Lang Eq. 3.1); `canonicalRotation` gives each one a key.

**Accordion** — a strip with alternating mountain and valley creases, so its facets stack in order. It folds whatever the widths (Lang §2.6). **End fold** — a flap folded over its neighbour by two creases of one assignment; it fits when the flap is no wider than the facet it lies on. **Spiral** — a strip with one assignment throughout and widths that do not grow; each turn nests inside the last. Tested with `parallelStrip` and the exact 1D oracle `stripFoldsFlat` in `core/solve/testing.ts`.

**Pleat word, hand** — with the two creases of every pleat opposite (anto), a twist's M/V assignment is read as a word of n bits, one per pleat (1 when the pleat's crease leaving its first corner is a mountain); its class up to rotation is a necklace. A regular twist has two **hands**, mirror images: `regularTwist` defaults to the one with pleat direction dₖ turned clockwise by α from each side (the shipped hexagon), and `mirror` gives the other (the shipped triangle). They fold alike (`core/gadgets/regular-twist.ts`, `core/generate/twist-sweep.ts`).

**Shrink-rotate, pleat orientation** — the map z ↦ c + λ(z − c) applied to every tile of a tiling about its centroid c turns the tiling into a twist tessellation (Lang §6.1–6.2); Kawasaki holds at every inner vertex (Thm 23). Each tiling edge shared by two tiles becomes a pleat of two opposite creases, and one Boolean per edge, its **orientation** (`mountainOnFirst`), picks which; the twist polygons' sides are then forced. On the 30° lattice only λ = (2 + ω)/3 and its conjugate work (`core/generate/shrink-rotate.ts`, `symmetric-motif.ts`, `repair.ts`).

**Birth set, disk guard, pleat rule** — the growth automaton of step 8.3 (`core/generate/growth.ts`) has one cell per tile of a regular tiling; a dead tile is born when its count of live side-neighbours is in the **birth set**, and live tiles never die. The live region must stay a topological disk (connected, V − E + F = 1), the **disk guard** (`isDisk`): a hole or pinch would make `foldFaces` or `shrinkRotate` throw. A **pleat rule** (`outward`, `inward`, `alternate`) sets every pleat's orientation from the two tiles' birth generations, so a grown pattern is a pure function of the seed, the rule and the generation count.

**Trackball camera** — the viewer's orbit as a rotation of the camera about the target in the camera's own axes, stored as a quaternion, so no direction is out of reach and there is no pole to flip over (V.1). The earlier turntable orbit clamped the polar angle. **Preset** — a named, ready-made crease pattern in the gallery (V.3). **Import snap** — rounding an imported file's float coordinates onto the exact lattice within a tolerance, or rejecting the file when it does not fit (V.5). **Material and Strain view** — the viewer's two colourings: the paper's front and back colours, or each bar coloured by its stretch (V.2a). **Grab** — a control mode where a drag pulls a mesh vertex through the simulation instead of orbiting (V.2b).

**Fold percent** — the fraction of a full fold the simulation asks of every crease: target angle = sign · percent · π, 0 flat, 100 percent fully folded, and in the viewer −100 percent the same with every mountain and valley swapped (V.2a). **Strain view** — colours each node by the mean stretch of its bars, blue for none to red at a 5 percent clip (V.2a). **Rigid mechanism** — a node displacement that stretches no bar and changes no dihedral angle; the nullspace of the compatibility matrix stacked with the fold-angle Jacobian, whose dimension is the number of rigid degrees of freedom (Schenk and Guest §3.2; V.10). **K_ratio** — the facet bending stiffness over the fold-line stiffness, the one dimensionless material parameter of a folded sheet (Schenk and Guest §3.3). **FOLD** — the JSON crease-pattern format of Demaine and Ku (`.fold`): `vertices_coords`, `edges_vertices`, `edges_assignment` (M, V, B, F, U, C), `faces_vertices` and an optional `edges_foldAngle` (V.5). **Developable** also appears in Tachi's form: zero Gauss area, 2π minus the sector sum, at every interior vertex.
