Reasoning checks are not enough here: random subsets almost never make a pinch, so the agreement test adds 1 to 3 arbitrary tiles to a random disk. **Birth {1} on 3⁶ stalls at 60 of 150 tiles** because the guard defers pinching births, and {2}, {3} never start from a hexagon seed. `grow`'s `history` has no empty entries; `status` says why it stopped (`stable`, `stalled`, `limit`). - **Shrink-rotate (8.2) lessons.** **Only λ = (2 + ω)/3 and its conjugate keep every crease on a 30° ray without a degenerate or crossing twist** (a brute force over μ/m, pinned in `shrink-rotate.test.ts`), so the family varies the tiling, window and orientation, not λ. **Every pleat orientation folds** (120 of 120 over 3⁶, 6³ and 3.6.3.6, both hands, up to 283 faces), so repair has nothing to do there; it works on raw per-crease assignments, where one flipped pleat crease is undone and a random 3⁶ radius-1 assignment reaches a full fold. Repair is slow on a heavily conflicted raw pattern (`minimumRepair`): 3.6.3.6 radius 3 is impractical, so tests use radius 2. A tiling edge's two shrunken copies are the two creases of one pleat (opposite), the polygon sides are forced by `solveMv`, and the outline is "B" (a boundary vertex with 3 or more tiles gets a closing chord, with 2 the single pleat is the outline). A rotation keeps M/V, so `edgeOrbits` flips an orbit member whose image lists its tiles in the other order, and throws when an edge maps to itself with its tiles swapped (a half turn about an edge midpoint). `vite-node` shifts `process.argv`, so pass scratch arguments through environment variables.
